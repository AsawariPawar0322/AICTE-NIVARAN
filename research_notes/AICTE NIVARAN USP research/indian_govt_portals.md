# Indian Government Public Grievance Redressal Portals: Features, Unique Capabilities, Documented Weaknesses (research as of Oct 2026)

Notes on method and source quality: many official pages (pib.gov.in, darpg.gov.in PDFs) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch, so several official figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages rather than full-page reads. They are marked "(search extract)". One long primary document, the DARPG occasional paper "Effective Redressal of Public Grievances" by V. Srinivas, IAS (Secretary DARPG, written around March–April 2023), was downloaded and read in full text; figures from it are marked "(DARPG paper, 2023)". Third-party "guide" sites (indiacustomercare, complainthub, jkgrievance, righttoinformation.wiki) are low-reliability and flagged where used.

## 1. CPGRAMS (pgportal.gov.in): current version, features, scale and performance

### Takeaway
CPGRAMS is a single national portal connecting all ~90 central ministries and the States/UTs, now on version 7.0. It auto-routes grievances to "last-mile" officers across 1,239 categories and 18,762 sub-categories. Around it sit a 10-step (later 14-step) reform package: AI/ML dashboards (IIT Kanpur), a BSNL feedback call centre, an appeal option that opens on a "Poor" rating, ministry rankings (GRAI), a Tree Dashboard of officer-wise pendency, and, since May 2026, a Bhashini voice chatbot ("Samadhan Didi"). Average disposal time is 15 days against a 21-day SLA, but the share of citizens satisfied with the outcome has stayed around 44–50% (Nov 2024), with a claimed 76% in H1 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Architecture and core features**
- CPGRAMS is "a single portal connected to all the Ministries/Departments of Government of India and States", with role-based access for every ministry and state. Citizens can use it through the web portal, a standalone Google Play app, and a mobile app integrated with UMANG. Status is tracked by a unique registration ID — [DARPG paper, 2023 (NCGG-hosted PDF)](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- DARPG guidelines (as of 2023): Grievance Redressal Officers (GROs) are designated down to the last-mile level, and Grievance Appellate Officers sit "one rank above" the GROs. The time limit was then 30 days for both grievances and appeals — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- Comprehensive guidelines of 23 Aug 2024 cut the resolution timeline to 21 days, with mandatory interim replies when resolution in time is not possible. Grievances "shall not be closed on the ground of incomplete information without making genuine effort to reach the complainant". If the information cannot be obtained, the case is closed into a separate category and is not sent for feedback — [NPS-CRA circular of 24.02.2025 on CPGRAMS resolution](https://www.npscra.proteantech.in/download/Circular%20dated%2024.02.2025%20resolution%20of%20grievances%20on%20CPGRAMS_Eng_Version.pdf); [DARPG Comprehensive Guidelines (pgportal)](https://pgportal.gov.in/Home/Preview/Q29tcHJlaGVuc2l2ZUd1aWRlbGluZXNGb3JIYW5kbGluZ1RoZVB1YmxpY0dyaWV2YW5jZXMucGRm)
- Further rules under the guidelines: a closure that rejects the citizen's request must give "valid, speaking reasons". Frivolous cases can be closed without a detailed Action Taken Report, and feedback is disabled for them (search extract) — [DARPG Comprehensive Guidelines](https://pgportal.gov.in/Home/Preview/Q29tcHJlaGVuc2l2ZUd1aWRlbGluZXNGb3JIYW5kbGluZ1RoZVB1YmxpY0dyaWV2YW5jZXMucGRm)
- Grievances may not be closed with the reason "does not pertain to this Ministry/Department". Misdirected grievances must be transferred to the right authority — [Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/speedy-grievances-redressal-under-cpgrams)
- Appeal flow: after closure, the complainant gives feedback. "If the rating is 'Poor' the option to file an appeal is enabled", and appeal status is tracked with the same registration number. The appeal mechanism was introduced in 2021 — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf); [IMPRI, June 2026](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/beyond-digital-box-ticking-a-critical-analysis-of-indias-cpgrams/)
- A disposed grievance "is treated as closed unless the citizen has filed the appeal" (search extract) — [DARPG Comprehensive Guidelines](https://pgportal.gov.in/Home/Preview/Q29tcHJlaGVuc2l2ZUd1aWRlbGluZXNGb3JIYW5kbGluZ1RoZVB1YmxpY0dyaWV2YW5jZXMucGRm)

**10-step reform (2022), plus 4 steps added in 2023**
- The reforms were adopted after the PM's review meeting of 16 Apr 2022. The 10 steps are: (1) universalisation of CPGRAMS 7.0, with auto-routing to the last mile; (2) AI/ML for automatic flagging of urgent grievances; (3) the portal in 22 scheduled languages plus English; (4) a Grievance Redressal Index ranking ministries; (5) a 50-seat Feedback Call Centre; (6) One Nation One Portal, integrating state and other GoI portals; (7) filing through Common Service Centres (CSCs); (8) Sevottam training; (9) monthly progress reports for the Centre and the States; (10) a Data Strategy Unit (DSU). After the Feb 2023 Chintan Shivir, four steps were added: (11) Citizen Connect, (12) strengthening existing grievance systems, (13) improving the quality of disposal, (14) re-imagining service delivery — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- Partners named: CSC, IIT Kanpur, NISG, NID Ahmedabad, QCI, C-DAC, BSNL, CGG Hyderabad, HIPA Gurgaon — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- CPGRAMS 7.0 widened grievance categories "from the broad set of 20 categories to 1239 major categories and 18762 sub-categories". It maps last-mile officers so grievances no longer pass step by step from the ministry nodal officer downward. Onboarding ran from the Dept of Posts (25.9.2019) to the Ministry of Shipping (6.10.2022), and the PMO PG portal was migrated in on 18.2.2023 — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- "The AI enabled grievance monitoring dashboards have been developed by IIT Kanpur which are used for identify spam, word search, repetitive grievances filed by habitual complainants." The paper also mentions an "Intelligent Grievance Management Dashboard leveraging AI/ML" — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- IGMS 2.0 uses AI/ML for "instant grievance analysis" (2025 annual report summary) — [The Policy Edge, CPGRAMS Annual Report 2025](https://www.policyedge.in/p/cpgrams-annual-report-2025-advancing)
- The DSU (set up March 2022) built a Tree Dashboard "to monitor grievance officer wise pendency in Central Ministries/Departments and in States/UTs". Officers with high pendency are named in the monthly reports. The DSU dashboard also provides GRO data analysis, trend, geographical and root-cause analysis, and scheme-wise monitoring (PMAY, Samagra Shiksha, Health & Wellness Centres, Har Ghar Jal) — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- Feedback Call Centre: run through BSNL since 1 July 2022. It takes feedback "on every Grievance that is redressed", covering both central and state grievances, with 50 language-based seats. Dissatisfied citizens can file an appeal through the call centre itself. By March 2023 it had reached ~3,68,425 citizens, of whom >1,10,000 rated the outcome Excellent/Very Good/Good — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- Monthly review regime: DARPG holds five monthly review meetings (GROs, appellate officers, knowledge partners, state nodal officers, state ATIs). Monthly reports go to every Secretary and Chief Secretary under a DO letter — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)

**Recent additions (2024–2026)**
- The CPGRAMS mobile app and GRAI 2023 were launched together with the October 2024 monthly reports — [Drishti IAS, GRAI 2023 (Nov 2024)](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/grievance-redressal-assessment-and-index-grai-2023/print_manually). The iOS app is listed on the App Store — [CPGRAMS App, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/cpgrams/id6746528698)
- GRAI 2023 assessed 89 central ministries/departments on 4 dimensions (efficiency, feedback, domain, GROs) and 11 indicators, using Jan–Dec 2023 CPGRAMS data. 85 of 89 improved on the previous year. Top in Groups A/B/C were the Dept of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, CAG and DIPAM. The index was devised on a Parliamentary Standing Committee recommendation (secondary source) — [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/grievance-redressal-assessment-and-index-grai-2023/print_manually); [IMPRI/search extract on 4 dimensions](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/beyond-digital-box-ticking-a-critical-analysis-of-indias-cpgrams/)
- 2025 annual report summary: >20 lakh grievances received (to Nov 2025); >1.1 lakh registered GROs; >10.7 lakh citizens gave feedback, 66% of it via the call centre. A Review Meeting Module enabled 223 senior-level meetings, which identified 52 systemic policy issues. 15 State/UT portals are fully integrated, and filing is possible at 5.6 lakh CSCs. GRAI evaluated ministries on 11 indicators — [The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/cpgrams-annual-report-2025-advancing)
- "Samadhan Didi": an AI voice chatbot built by DARPG with Bhashini and launched by MoS Dr Jitendra Singh on 30 May 2026. It takes a spoken complaint, asks clarifying questions, identifies the ministry, category and sub-category, and files the grievance. Officials say it supports the 22 scheduled languages, with Bhojpuri, Garo, Khasi, Mizo and Bodo planned. Sources disagree on whether all 22 are live now — [AIR News, 30 May 2026](https://newsonair.gov.in/union-minister-jitendra-singh-launches-ai-enabled-cpgrams-voice-chatbot-samadhan-didi/); [OpenGov Asia](https://opengovasia.com/india-introduces-ai-voice-chatbot-samadhan-didi-to-expand-access-to-public-grievance-system/)
- A 2026 PIB release describes "AI-based validation" meant to judge resolution quality and flag cases "closed by transfer or without effective resolution". It also describes a planned "NextGen CPGRAMS" with automated escalation and real-time analytics dashboards (search extract; full page 403) — [PIB PRID 2296724](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296724&reg=48&lang=1)

**Volume, disposal time and satisfaction statistics**
- Grievances received and disposed in-year (all CPGRAMS): 2018 15.86 lakh (78.72%); 2019 18.68 lakh (77.49%); 2020 22.71 lakh (83.20%); 2021 20.01 lakh (83.23%); 2022 19.18 lakh (85.64%). State-level in-year disposal was much lower: 43.95% (2018), 45.47% (2019), 58.84% (2020), 57.81% (2021), 70.03% (2022) — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- Average disposal time for central ministries: 32 days (2021), 27 days (2022), 16 days (Jan–Mar 2023) — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf). It was 157 days in 2014 and 15 days in 2025 per PIB (search extract) — [PIB PRID 2296724](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296724&reg=48&lang=1)
- As of 31.01.2026: average disposal time 15 days vs a prescribed 21 days; 71,887 grievances pending with central ministries, of which 5,845 had been pending more than 90 days (Parliament answer, search extract) — [PIB PRID 2226247](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226247&reg=3&lang=1)
- Pending at 28.02.2025: 59,946 cases with ministries, 63.86% of them pending under 21 days. Since 2019, >1.15 crore grievances (search extract of PIB post) — [PIB India on X](https://x.com/PIB_India/status/1907396717655548293)
- A record 26,45,869 grievances were redressed in 2024, and 1,15,52,503 over 2020–2024 — [IMPRI, June 2026](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/beyond-digital-box-ticking-a-critical-analysis-of-indias-cpgrams/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Frecord-over-26-lakh-public-grievances-redressed-in-2024-union-minister-jitendra-singh-3453011)
- October 2025: >1.44 lakh grievances redressed by central ministries, with an average of 15 days — [AIR News, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/central-ministries-resolve-over-1-44-lakh-grievances-in-october-cpgrams-report). September 2025: 1.66 lakh — [Prameya News](https://www.prameyanews.com/166-lakh-grievances-redressed-in-september-by-central-ministries-departments)
- Appeals: the average closing time for appeals at central ministries was 25 days (1 Jan–28 Mar 2025) — [DARPG Monthly Report No. 35, March 2025](https://www.darpg.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/5abf640eeedd0b573aa4e0329d5016a2.pdf) (search extract)
- Satisfaction: the November 2024 monthly report says 44% of citizens who gave feedback were satisfied (50% for central ministries). For SBI, 48% were dissatisfied vs 46% satisfied (search extract) — [DARPG CPGRAMS Monthly Report](https://www.darpg.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/815f2fafc74539f9705d53b1f466f25e.pdf). PIB claims 76% citizen satisfaction for Jan–Jun 2026 (search extract) — [PIB PRID 2296724](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296724&reg=48&lang=1)
- At CSCs: 44.5% of grievances received by the Dept of Agriculture were filed through CSCs, and PM-KISAN was the top category (2023) — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)

### Inferences
- CPGRAMS's "intelligence" points mainly at the government side: spam and habitual-complainant detection, pendency trees, ministry rankings and review meetings. Its citizen-facing loop is thin: a closure, then an after-the-fact rating, then an appeal only if the rating is "Poor".
- The core design flaw is that closure comes first and verification after. The GRO closes, and the citizen can only react through a rating or appeal. The appeal is the only remedy and is handled within the same hierarchy (one rank above). AICTE NIVARAN's OTP-confirmed closure, where the institution cannot close unilaterally, reverses this order. No evidence was found that CPGRAMS requires complainant consent before closure.
- CPGRAMS is officer- and ministry-centred. Grievances are counted per officer and per ministry, not per respondent entity. The IIT-K dashboard spots "repetitive grievances", but treats them as spam or habitual complaining rather than as a pattern against one respondent. NIVARAN's institution-centred record keyed by AICTE Permanent ID, with joint hearings for linked complaints, has no visible CPGRAMS equivalent.
- The government's own move toward "AI-based validation" to catch fake closures (2026) and the Review Meeting Module's "systemic issues" (52 found) show that both problems are recognised as unsolved.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full 2026 PIB releases (HTTP 403), so the "76% satisfaction" and "AI-based validation" claims are unverified beyond search extracts.
- No public figure found for the share of grievances appealed or the share of appeals that overturn the original closure.
- No primary source found on whether CPGRAMS supports in-portal two-way messaging between citizen and GRO. From the materials, communication appears limited to clarification requests and the final ATR. Unverified.
- No CPGRAMS statistics found for the Department of Higher Education or for AICTE specifically.
- "IGMS 2.0" details are only from a secondary summary of the 2025 annual report.

## 2. AICTE's own grievance mechanism today

### Takeaway
AICTE has no single public, case-tracked portal for grievances against approved institutions. Its mechanism is regulatory and decentralised. Students must first go to the institution's own Student Grievance Redressal Committee (under the AICTE student grievance regulations), then appeal within 15 days to an Ombudsperson appointed by the affiliating university, the state DTE, or (for PGDM institutes) AICTE. AICTE's central Grievance Redressal Bureau takes complaints by email (complaint@aicte-india.org) and through a "Centralized Support System". Each college is required to run its own online grievance portal, which has created a fragmented market of per-college portals.

### Cited Findings
- The AICTE student grievance regulations require complaints to go first to the institution's Student Grievance Redressal Committee. "Any student aggrieved by the decision of the Student Grievance Redressal Committee may prefer an appeal to the Ombudsperson, within a period of fifteen days from the date of receipt of such decision" — [Careers360, "Appoint 'Ombudsperson' for student complaints: AICTE to institutions"](https://news.careers360.com/appoint-ombudsperson-for-student-complaints-aicte-institutions)
- Ombudsperson appointment varies by institution type. Affiliating, technical, private and deemed universities appoint one under UGC rules. The state DTE appoints one for diploma institutions. AICTE appointed former IIM Lucknow director Devi Singh for AICTE-approved PGDM institutions — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/appoint-ombudsperson-for-student-complaints-aicte-institutions)
- College pages list the AICTE PGDM Ombudsperson as "C/o Public Grievance Redressal Cell, AICTE, Nelson Mandela Marg, New Delhi-110070, complaint@aicte-india.org" (institution-hosted, unverified on AICTE's site) — [KCG College ombudsman page](https://kcgcollege.ac.in/ombudsman-grievance-redressal/); [MREC Ombudsman PDF](https://www.mrec.ac.in/MREC_GRIEVANCE/Ombudsman.pdf)
- AICTE has a Grievance Redressal Bureau page on both aicte-india.org and aicte.gov.in. It handles grievances, complaints and malpractices from students, faculty and other stakeholders, with contact complaint@aicte-india.org. Filing is via css.aicte-india.org ("Centralized Support System") after registration, per a third-party mirror. Note: aicte.gov.in/bureaus/grievance-redressal and aicte.gov.in/centralized-support-system returned 404 on direct fetch in Oct 2026 — [AICTE Grievance Redressal (aicte-india.org)](https://www.aicte-india.org/bureaus/grievance-redressal); [AICTE Centralized Support System](https://aicte.gov.in/centralized-support-system); [PVG COET mirror of AICTE instructions](https://www.pvgcoet.ac.in/students-corner/grievance-redressal-systems-by-aicte/)
- AICTE-approved colleges run their own online grievance submission pages to comply with AICTE norms (e.g., IRMA, VNRVJIET's EduPrime portal, MICA, KGEC). A commercial vendor sells "Grievance Portal as per AICTE Norms" — [IRMA](https://irma.ac.in/online-grievance-submission); [VNRVJIET PDF](https://vnrvjiet.ac.in/assets/pdfs/home/Grievance.pdf); [grievanceportals.com](http://grievanceportals.com/)
- Sources disagree on which regulation year applies (2012 vs 2019). Institutions cite both — [search synthesis of college pages; e.g. IMDR Grievance PDF](https://www.imdr.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Grievance-Redressal.pdf)

### Inferences
- The AICTE regulator sees only a slice of complaints: those emailed or raised through CSS, plus whatever reaches an ombudsperson. Institution-level committee cases sit in hundreds of separate college portals with no shared ID and no AICTE-level aggregation by Permanent ID. That is the gap AICTE NIVARAN's institution-centred, regulator-level record directly fills.
- Because the first tier is the institution's own committee, the respondent effectively judges its own case. That conflict of interest is a strong argument for NIVARAN's "institution cannot close unilaterally" rule and its ombudsman escalation.

### Gaps
- Could not load AICTE's current grievance pages (404) to confirm whether CSS has ticket tracking, SLAs or status visibility. No published AICTE grievance volume or disposal statistics found.
- Could not confirm whether AICTE grievances flow into CPGRAMS (Ministry of Education / Dept of Higher Education) or are handled separately.
- Exact current regulation (AICTE Student Grievance Redressal Regulations 2019 vs later amendments) not verified from the AICTE gazette.

## 3. UGC e-Samadhaan (samadhaan.ugc.ac.in)

### Takeaway
UGC e-Samadhaan is a single-window portal for grievances from students, teaching and non-teaching staff, and HEIs. It merged all earlier UGC portals and helplines except the Anti-Ragging Helpline. It assigns a Unique Grievance ID that routes to the concerned UGC bureau head, and publishes category SLAs: 20 working days for students and HEIs, 15 for staff. It flags non-responding institutions with reminders. It has a Fee Nivaran Cell with outcome data on refunds, but no published appeal stage.

### Cited Findings
- Stakeholders are students, teaching and non-teaching staff, HEIs, nodal officers and UGC bureau heads. Filing is online, by email or by toll-free number. Each complaint gets a Unique Grievance ID that appears in the assigned officer's account — [UGC e-Samadhaan About Us](https://samadhaan.ugc.ac.in/Pages/AboutUs)
- Timelines: student grievances 20 working days; teaching/non-teaching staff 15 working days; HEI grievances 20 working days. Ragging and gender issues are handled under existing norms with a 24x7 helpline — [UGC e-Samadhaan About Us](https://samadhaan.ugc.ac.in/Pages/AboutUs)
- Escalation: the system flags institutions that don't respond and sends them reminders, and the Commission may "take appropriate action". No appeal process is described on the page — [UGC e-Samadhaan About Us](https://samadhaan.ugc.ac.in/Pages/AboutUs)
- Helplines: toll-free 1800-111-656 (24x7); Anti-Ragging 1800-180-5522, kept separate; tech support 011-23604502 — [UGC e-Samadhaan About Us](https://samadhaan.ugc.ac.in/Pages/AboutUs)
- Scope reported in the press: non-refund of fees, withheld original certificates, merit admission, and refusal of admission against stated policy. The portal was announced for launch around Aug–Sept 2022 — [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/ugc-to-launch-e-samadhan-portal-in-september-to-resolve-grievances-of-students-156579.html); [Kerala Kaumudi, Aug 2022](https://keralakaumudi.com/web-news/en/2022/08/NMAN0356679/1.html)
- Fee Nivaran Cell (within e-Samadhaan, started 2020): resolved 4,257 fee complaints over five academic years and refunded Rs 25.51 crore, with a ~97% refund rate claimed by UGC Chair M. Jagadesh Kumar. >50% of complaints came in 2023-24. Complaints came from 32 States/UTs, led by UP, Delhi and Rajasthan, and private universities had the most complaints (article dated 22 Jan 2025) — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/ugcs-fee-nivaran-cell-resolves-over-4-thousand-complaints-refunds-rs-25-crore-to-students)

### Inferences
- e-Samadhaan's strongest notable feature is the Fee Nivaran Cell's outcome metric: rupees refunded, a measure of actual resolution rather than closure count. NIVARAN could adopt "outcome achieved" metrics (refund issued, certificate returned) and not just closures.
- e-Samadhaan is still a complaint-to-UGC-bureau model. "Flag and remind" is its only enforcement lever, there is no complainant-confirmed closure, and no appeal is documented.

### Gaps
- No total e-Samadhaan volume, disposal or satisfaction statistics found.
- No independent reviews, audits or news critiques of e-Samadhaan found in this search budget.
- Not confirmed whether e-Samadhaan supports document upload integrity, in-portal messaging or Hindi/regional languages.

## 4. State-level portals with notable features

### Takeaway
State systems are where the most interesting accountability mechanisms live. These include outbound feedback calls before or after closure, reopening on a low rating, monthly published top-10/bottom-10 rankings of districts and officers (UP), multi-level escalation (L1 to L4) with disciplinary action for "force closure" (MP CM Helpline 181), "reality check" verification of disposed cases (Rajasthan Sampark), and face-to-face hearings at four tiers (Gujarat SWAGAT). WhatsApp channels are also appearing (MP 181, Andhra Mana Mitra). The same states show the failure modes: Andhra PGRS surveys found large dissatisfaction with "resolved" cases.

### Cited Findings

**Uttar Pradesh: IGRS / Jansunwai-Samadhan (jansunwai.up.nic.in, helpline 1076)**
- Run by NIC for the CM's Office, with a 24-hour toll-free helpline at 1076. Citizens submit a satisfaction rating via "Send Feedback" using the grievance ID and mobile number. A low rating can reopen the case (third-party guide) — [UP Jansunwai Play Store app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.nic.up.jansunwai.upjansunwai&hl=en); [jkgrievance.in guide](https://jkgrievance.in/up-jansunwai-guide/); [IndiaFilings](https://www.indiafilings.com/learn/uttar-pradesh-jansunwai-portal)
- A third-party claim that an "Unsatisfied" rating auto-escalates to the next higher officer, and that the call centre handles ~80,000 inbound and 55,000 outbound calls daily with feedback on 100% of redressed complaints. Not verified from an official source — [jkgrievance.in](https://jkgrievance.in/up-jansunwai-guide/); [indiacustomercare](https://g.indiacustomercare.com/uttar-pradesh-cm-jansunwai-helpline-guide)
- Monthly public rankings: the CM reviews top-10 and bottom-10 lists of DMs, police chiefs and tehsils across Jansunwai-Samadhan, IGRS and the CM Helpline. For example, in August 2023 Amethi, Kannauj and Shravasti were top and Baghpat, Gorakhpur and Bareilly bottom. Poor performers face review and accountability (dated 2023) — [ANI, 17 Sep 2023](https://www.aninews.in/news/national/general-news/up-govt-announces-list-of-top-worst-rankings-of-jansunwai-igrs-for-august20230917161514/)

**Madhya Pradesh: CM Helpline 181 (cmhelpline.mp.gov.in)**
- Channels: 181 phone, a WhatsApp number (+91 755 2555582), email and a mobile app linked to the 181 portal (third-party listing) — [indiacustomercare MP guide](https://g.indiacustomercare.com/cm-helpline-madhya-pradesh); [CM Helpline citizens app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.magnum.helpline&hl=en)

It won the 2010 UN Public Service Award — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest anti-false-closure practices in India are state innovations: MP's punishment for force closure, Rajasthan's reality checks of disposed cases, and UP's reopening on a low rating plus public district rankings. All of them are checks *after* closure, done by the government. NIVARAN's complainant OTP gate *before* closure is a structurally different (preventive) control. It can be pitched as "digitising MP's force-closure rule into the workflow itself".
- Public league tables (UP top-10/bottom-10) are a proven pressure tool. NIVARAN's institution compliance view could become a public-facing institution scorecard keyed by AICTE Permanent ID.
- SWAGAT's live hearing model parallels NIVARAN's joint hearing queue for linked complaints against the same institution.
- WhatsApp and voice intake (MP 181, AP Mana Mitra, CPGRAMS Samadhan Didi) is becoming table stakes in 2026.

### Gaps

- MP 181 L1–L4 level definitions and official satisfaction numbers not verified from primary sources.
- UP IGRS call-centre volumes and auto-escalation rules come from low-reliability guide sites only.
- No current (2025–26) UP ranking list found; the ANI example is from 2023.

## 5. Documented weaknesses (audits, parliamentary committees, DARPG's own data, news, citizen reviews)

### Takeaway
The weakness that recurs across sources is "disposal is not resolution". Fast closure numbers (15 days) coexist with satisfaction around 44–50% (CPGRAMS, Nov 2024) and, in Andhra Pradesh, surveys finding 78–81% dissatisfaction among "resolved" cases. Other documented problems: templated "forwarded to concerned department" replies counted as resolved; forwarding loops between Centre, State and local bodies; closures that just tell citizens to approach another agency; each complaint treated in isolation, hiding systemic patterns; accountability kept inside the same cadre with no independent ombudsman; and digital-divide exclusion.

### Cited Findings
- Parliamentary Standing Committee (111th report, via secondary summary): flagged grievances disposed of "without proper closure, only with suggestions to visit another agency", and recommended that ministries give valid reasons for closure. It also flagged that state-matter grievances were sometimes disposed of instead of forwarded to the states — [Anantam IAS notes](https://anantamias.com/grievance-redressal-mechanism/)
- The 127th Report (tabled 15 Mar 2023) praised the 10-step reforms and the GRI, and urged DARPG to keep meeting with underperforming departments. DARPG says it acted on 36 PSC recommendations in 2021–23 — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- The 110th Report of the committee on pensioner grievances (CPENGRAMS) examined effectiveness and the Pension Adalats (2022; blog reproduction) — [blog reproduction of 110th Report Ch. 2](https://scm-bps.blogspot.com/2022/02/effectiveness-of-cpengrams-and-pension.html)
- DARPG's own data: only 44% of feedback-givers were satisfied in November 2024 (50% for central ministries). For SBI, dissatisfied (48%) outnumbered satisfied (46%) — [DARPG Monthly Report (search extract)](https://www.darpg.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/815f2fafc74539f9705d53b1f466f25e.pdf)
- Before 2023, in-year state disposal on CPGRAMS was only 44–70% (2018–2022), so carry-forward backlogs were large — [DARPG paper, 2023](https://ncgg.org.in/sites/default/files/lectures-document/Effective-Public-Grievance-Redressal-Occassional-Paper-of-DARPG.pdf)
- 5,845 of 71,887 pending central grievances were older than 90 days, against a 21-day norm, as on 31.01.2026 — [PIB PRID 2226247 (search extract)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2226247&reg=3&lang=1)
- IMPRI critique (Yashkirti Pal, 19 Jun 2026):
  - The 21-day target fosters a "disposal-at-all-costs" culture, and departments are rewarded for closing tickets. Generic replies such as "matter forwarded to concerned department" or "noted for future action" get marked resolved.
  - Forwarding loops (central to state to local) recreate bureaucracy.
  - Multiple complaints about the same systemic issue are logged as separate cases, which hides patterns.
  - Monitoring officers come from the same cadre as those complained against ("internalised accountability" without an independent ombudsman), and there is a lack of "consequence architecture".
  - Digital-divide exclusion of rural and elderly citizens.
  
  This is opinion and analysis with limited systematic data — [IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/policy-update/beyond-digital-box-ticking-a-critical-analysis-of-indias-cpgrams/)
- Citizen reviews on the CPGRAMS App Store page: complaints "unilaterally closed without any reply to the complainant" over the last ~2 years; "many complaints appear to be closed without satisfactory action" (anecdotal) — [CPGRAMS App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/cpgrams/id6746528698)
- Forum case: a CPGRAMS complaint filed in Feb 2025 was still pending in Aug 2025. Responders advised using Appeal/Escalate and filing an RTI to get the Action Taken Report. This implies complainants lack visibility into what action was taken (anecdotal) — [ComplaintHub forum](https://citizen.complainthub.org/t/delayed-in-redressal-of-public-grievance-on-cpgrams-portal/26534)
- Andhra Pradesh PGRS: a government-commissioned IVRS survey found 78% of complainants dissatisfied with the process. An independent IVRS survey of 33,538 "resolved" cases reportedly found only 22% satisfaction, and 81% dissatisfaction for cases handled by the CM's office. The Wire documented pensions marked "resolved" without action (headline: "Too Many Delays, False Closures…"). Figures come from search extracts, as the article body could not be fetched — [The Wire](https://thewire.in/politics/too-many-delays-false-closures-why-andhras-public-grievance-redressal-system-is-facing-backlash). The government disputed the report — [The Wire, AP govt response](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/andhra-pradesh-govt-responds-to-the-wire-report-claims-public-grievances-being-addressed-effectively)
- MP: the very need to suspend an officer for "force (wrong) closure" shows that false closure is a known pattern there — [MP CM Helpline media compilation](https://cmhelpline.mp.gov.in/PDF/CM_Helpline_voice_of_media.pdf)
- A 2026 advocacy blog argues that the focus on statistical speed yields "arbitrary resolutions" lacking genuine inquiry (opinion, unclear sourcing) — [yogi.systems, Sep 2026](https://yogi.systems/2026/09/25/grievance-disposal-on-cpgram-portal-explained/)
- Integration gap: CPGRAMS complaints may not reach state utilities that use separate state portals (Haryana example). As of 2025, only 15 State/UT portals were fully integrated — [righttoinformation.wiki (low reliability)](https://righttoinformation.wiki/state-grievance-portals-comparison-india-2026); [The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/cpgrams-annual-report-2025-advancing)

### Inferences
Mapping documented weaknesses to NIVARAN features (candidate USP framing):
- *Closure without resolution / unilateral closure* → NIVARAN's complainant OTP-confirmed closure, where the institution cannot close on its own. No surveyed portal makes complainant consent a precondition of closure. They all verify after the fact, through a call, rating, appeal or reality check.
- *Same-cadre accountability, no independent ombudsman* → NIVARAN escalates to an Ombudsman. AICTE regulations already provide for an ombudsperson, but it is offline and outside any portal.
- *Duplicate complaints hide systemic issues* → NIVARAN's "link to existing complaint" and joint hearing queue, keyed by AICTE Permanent ID. CPGRAMS uses repetition only to flag "habitual complainants".
- *No visibility of action taken; RTI needed to see the ATR* → NIVARAN's actor-attributed timeline and case-linked chat.
- *Templated replies* → could add a "substantive response" check, e.g. block closure if the response matches boilerplate, or require evidence attachments with SHA-256 hashes.
- *Forwarding loops* → NIVARAN's single respondent (the institution) and single regulator avoid the Centre-State-local loop, though AICTE vs university vs DTE jurisdiction still needs clear routing.
- Gaps NIVARAN does not yet cover, based on what others do: outbound feedback calls (CPGRAMS, UP), public district or institution league tables (UP, GRAI), WhatsApp and voice intake (MP, AP, Samadhan Didi), and outcome metrics such as rupees refunded (UGC Fee Nivaran).

### Gaps
- No CAG audit of CPGRAMS or of any state grievance portal was found within the search budget.
- No Indian Express, ThePrint, The Hindu or Medianama articles specifically on CPGRAMS closure quality were returned by the searches. That coverage may exist but was not found.
- No quantitative study found measuring the share of CPGRAMS closures that are "forwarded" or templated responses.
- The 111th PSC report text was not read directly; only a secondary summary was used.
